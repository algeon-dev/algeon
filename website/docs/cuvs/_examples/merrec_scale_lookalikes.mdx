### Can a lookalike audience find next season's Pokemon buyers?

The seed audience is every shopper who bought a Pokemon listing in July or
August 2023. The question is whether shoppers who browse like them buy
Pokemon in September and October, the following two months. Each shopper's
taste vector is the square root of their view share across the 64 busiest
second-level categories, computed only from views before September 1.
Shoppers need at least 20 such views. Exact kNN returns the 20 nearest
non-seed shoppers for each seed buyer, and SQL compares later purchase
rates for three audiences:

- the lookalikes;
- every non-seed shopper who viewed a Pokemon listing before September;
- every non-seed shopper with a taste vector.

| Audience | Shoppers | Bought Pokemon Sep-Oct | Purchase rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| lookalikes | 19,125 | 132 | 0.690% |
| pokemon viewers | 250,627 | 572 | 0.228% |
| all active shoppers | 2,079,441 | 688 | 0.033% |

The lookalike audience bought Pokemon at 21 times the rate of all active
shoppers and three times the rate of everyone who had viewed Pokemon. For a
retargeting budget, that is a 19,000-shopper list in place of a
250,000-shopper one, with a higher share of later buyers. A CPU reproduction shows where the lift comes from. The 13,264 lookalikes
who had viewed a Pokemon listing before September bought at 0.98%; the 5,859
who had not bought at 0.034%, the base rate. The vectors rank shoppers who
already show interest; they do not find new interest. Purchase counts here are small: 132 lookalikes
bought. Equal distances let the 20th neighbor vary, so the lookalike count
was 19,122 or 19,125 across the three captured runs; the buyer count stayed at 132.

The taste vectors are 64 wide `REAL` columns. SQL-built list columns are
nullable, and cuVS requires non-null vectors, so each dimension is its own
column.
