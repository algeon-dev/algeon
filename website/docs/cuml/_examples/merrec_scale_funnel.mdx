The campaign list holds 5,000 shoppers. One query builds candidate lists
with a graph algorithm, a vector search and an externally trained model, then
scores every list against the purchases that followed.

The seed is the {n(1302)} shoppers who bought a Pokemon listing in July or
August 2023. Eligible shoppers are the {n(byStage[1].shoppers)} others in
`mr_taste64`, the prepared table of 64-dimensional taste vectors for shoppers
with at least 20 views before September 1. Three stages read that table and
the raw events:

- **Graph affinity.** `cugraph_personalized_pagerank` starts from the Pokemon
  brand vertex on the user-brand like graph built from likes before September.
  The 20,000 eligible shoppers with the highest scores form the graph audience.
- **Taste lookalikes.** `cuvs_brute_force_knn` returns the 20 nearest taste
  neighbors of each seed buyer among eligible shoppers.
- **Model ranking.** `cuml_forest_predict` scores every eligible shopper with
  `propensity_xgb.json`, the XGBoost purchase-propensity model from the
  [buyer shortlist example](#model). Within each audience, the 5,000 highest
  scores form a shortlist.

Window functions rank the scores inside each audience in one pass, and the
final `GROUP BY` counts the shoppers in each list who bought a Pokemon listing
in September or October.

Previous viewers buy at {pct(byStage[2])}, seven times the
{pct(byStage[1], 3)} rate of all eligible shoppers. The graph
audience reaches {pct(byStage[3])} and the taste lookalikes {pct(byStage[4])}.
The two lists share only {n(byStage[3].shoppers + byStage[4].shoppers - byStage[5].shoppers)} shoppers,
so each method finds people the other misses. Ranking their union with the
model and keeping 5,000 gives {pct(byStage[8])}: {n(byStage[8].pokemon_buyers_sep_oct)} of
the pool's {n(byStage[5].pokemon_buyers_sep_oct)} buyers in
{(100 * byStage[8].shoppers / byStage[5].shoppers).toFixed(0)}% of its shoppers.

The two control rows show what the algorithms add. The model alone, applied
to everyone, reaches {pct(byStage[6])}. Trained on any purchase, with no
brand among its features, it ranks shoppers without a Pokemon signal. The
viewer rule plus the model reaches
{pct(byStage[7])}. The graph-or-taste pool plus the model reaches
{n(byStage[8].pokemon_buyers_sep_oct - byStage[7].pokemon_buyers_sep_oct)} more
buyers than the rule at the same list size.

To hand the list to a campaign service, replace the final aggregation with a
projection over the `ranked` CTE. The timings above measure the evaluation
query, not this variant.

```sql
SELECT id AS user_id, rank_pool AS priority
FROM ranked
WHERE in_pool = 1
  AND rank_pool <= 5000
ORDER BY rank_pool;
```
