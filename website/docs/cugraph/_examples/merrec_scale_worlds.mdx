A merchandising analyst wants to find brands with overlapping audiences,
including combinations that cross catalog categories. The query uses
May to October 2023 likes: a fan is a distinct shopper who liked a brand.

SQL reduces 182,889,825 like events to user-brand pairs and selects the
5,000 brands with the most fans. Jaccard measures shared audience divided
by combined audience for each brand pair. Similarities of at least 0.02
become edges for Leiden; the resulting groups join back to brand names.
Leiden uses the retained connections and currently ignores edge weights.

The 101-brand group puts power tools beside kitchen appliances. It gives
an analyst a candidate home-project collection to inspect. Shared likes
supply a relationship beyond the catalog's categories; they do not establish
that the products substitute for one another or that a collection will sell.

The composition matters because Jaccard's output defines Leiden's input
inside the query. Date filters and the similarity threshold can change with
the question, without preparing a separate brand graph. Membership stability
across months has not been tested.
