The exclusion list came from a decision model. {excludedInTop20} of the
first 20 entries in the published ranking are retailers, house brands or
catch-all labels, and
betweenness is a property of the paths through them, so deleting them from
the output changes nothing for the other brands. The graph has to be rebuilt
without them.

A context query joined each of the 120 ranked entries to its catalog
footprint: listings, distinct first and second level categories, the three
largest category paths, mean price and four sample titles. A hosted decision
model answered three questions per entry from that context alone, with no
graph numbers in the prompt: the kind of entry (standalone brand,
multi-brand retailer, superstore house brand, generic catch-all), the
probability that the entry is a standalone brand, and a 0 to 3 pollution
score. The application's policy accepts an entry when the probability is at
least {bridges.policy.accept_min.toFixed(2)} and the kind is `standalone_brand`,
rejects it below {bridges.policy.review_min.toFixed(2)}, and sends the rest to
review. The answers for all 120 entries became a Parquet file, registered as
`brand_ontology`, and the bridges query ran again with one change in its
first CTE: entries the policy rejected or held for review leave the fan
graph.

Samsung's betweenness doubles in the purified graph. Conair and Black & Decker move from the sixties
into the top 10, and Bella enters a top 15 it was absent from. Post-filtering
keeps 11 of these 15 brands but in a different order and cannot place Bella.
The full 202-row table with every brand that appears in any of the three
rankings is `windows.all.ranking` in the data file; the {all.review_queue.length}-entry
review queue of both passes, with its context, is `windows.all.review_queue`. Claire's is there with
a standalone probability of 0.89 and a kind of `multi_brand_retailer` at
0.98, the kind of disagreement the review band holds.

Two controls. A hand-written denylist of {all.denylist_vs_model.denylist_size} retailer
and catch-all names, run through the same recompute, excludes
{all.denylist_vs_model.overlap} of the same entries; the model also excludes
{all.denylist_vs_model.only_model.length} the list missed and keeps
{all.denylist_vs_model.only_denylist.length} the list names. The two purified rankings
share 13 of their top 15. On the September to October window the model's
answers are reused from the first run: {sepOct.totals.registry_hits} of
{sepOct.totals.entities} lookups hit the registry, the remaining
{sepOct.totals.api_calls} calls cost ${sepOct.totals.cost_usd.toFixed(4)}, and
the model excludes {sepOct.denylist_vs_model.only_model.length} entries the denylist
does not know, among them Amazon, Sephora and QVC, while
{sepOct.denylist_vs_model.denylist_unmatched.length} of the list's names do not
appear in that window's top 120 at all.
