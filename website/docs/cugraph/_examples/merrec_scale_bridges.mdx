Betweenness centrality counts how often a brand lies on the shortest paths
between other brands in the similarity graph.
Brands that connect many worlds are candidates for cross-world navigation,
or for exclusion from a collection that should stay inside one world.

The SQL builds the same Jaccard graph as the worlds query: 5,000
brands, edges where the shared-fan similarity reaches 0.02. Leiden assigns
the worlds and `cugraph_betweenness_centrality` scores the same edges. A
join of each brand's graph neighbors to their world labels counts how many
worlds the brand touches directly.

The connectors are mostly sellers and generic labels that the catalog
stores in its brand field. Department stores and retailers (Walmart,
JCPenney, Macy's, Claire's), house brands (Mainstays, Better Homes &
Gardens) and catch-all entries (As Seen on TV, Customized & Personalized,
Eyeshadow, US Vintage) hold most of the first fifteen positions, each with
neighbors in 37 to 44 of the worlds. Samsung is the manufacturer with the
highest score. Its 98 neighbors span 13 worlds. To isolate manufacturer
brands from retail aggregators, exclude retailer and catch-all labels in the
initial CTE and rerun the centrality query.

Betweenness depends on the graph the query defines. A different
similarity threshold changes which paths exist, and a brand with few
neighbors can score highly when it is the only link between two worlds. The
world counts use the Leiden labels of the same execution.
