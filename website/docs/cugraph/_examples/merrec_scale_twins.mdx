export const n = value => value.toLocaleString('en-US');

Sellers can enter the same brand under several names. Two entries liked by
the same people are candidates for one brand. The input is every `item_like`
event of the six months, reduced to distinct user-brand pairs.

Jaccard divides the shared fans of two entries by the union of their fan
sets. The query scores all {n(1999000)} pairs of the 2,000 most-liked
brands in one call, then joins names and fan counts and keeps the 30 highest
scores.

The table shows the first 14 of {capture.rows} rows. The query

Most of the highest-scoring pairs are one brand entered twice: TXT and
Tomorrow X Together, Air Jordan and Jordan, Mugler and Thierry Mugler, Yves
Saint Laurent and its YSL variant, Christian Dior and Dior, A Bathing Ape
and Bape, Hermès and HERMES, BabyBjorn and BABYBJÖRN. No name was compared
to find them. The remaining pairs are distinct brands that one audience
follows together, such as Hello Kitty and Sanrio, Konami and Yu-Gi-Oh!, or
Panini and Topps.

The pair list is a SQL relation, so the same query could join each
pair's leaf categories or price ranges to separate duplicates from
companions. Scores over the full six months hide seasonal pairs; a date
filter on the events restricts the window.
