Which of those pairs are one brand? A decision model answered for the
120 highest-scoring pairs. The pair list is the query above with the fan
graph restricted to entries the [bridges follow-up](#bridges) accepted as
standalone brands, so retailers and catch-all labels pair with nothing, and
the limit raised from 30 to 120. For each pair the model saw the Jaccard
score and both entries' listings, mean price, two largest category paths and
three sample titles, and answered three questions: the relationship
(`same_entity_alias`, `sub_brand_or_diffusion_line`,
`ip_franchise_and_owner_maker`, `distinct_co_liked_peers`), the probability
that the two ids should merge, and a 0 to 3 alignment score. The policy
accepts a merge at a probability of {twins.policy.accept_min.toFixed(2)} or more
when the relationship is `same_entity_alias`, rejects it below
{twins.policy.review_min.toFixed(2)}, and sends the rest to review.

{twins.band_counts.accept} pairs were accepted, {twins.band_counts.review} sent to
review and {twins.band_counts.reject} rejected ({decide.api_calls} calls,
${decide.cost_usd.toFixed(4)}, {decide.wall_s.toFixed(1)} s). The rejects include the
pairs a fan-overlap rule alone would merge: Rilakkuma and San-X, Hello
Kitty and Sanrio, Konami and Yu-Gi-Oh!, Panini and Topps. The review queue
holds Hourglass Cosmetics and Hourglass at 0.69, Beats by Dr. Dre and Beats
at 0.68, and the sub-brand pairs Air Jordan and Jordan, Polo Ralph Lauren
and Ralph Lauren.

Accepted pairs are edges. `cugraph_weakly_connected_components` gives each
entry the smallest brand id of its component as a canonical id, and a scan
of every like event counts, per canonical brand, the exact number of
distinct users who liked any member. Every merged brand has two ids; the
table compares the fans of the id with more of them against the distinct
count over both, and the share of the summed count that is the same people
twice.

These are exact counts over all 182.9 million like events. Whichever id a

A string control proposes pairs by word-trigram similarity over all
{n(trigram.brand_names)} names, with a threshold of {trigram.threshold} (pg_trgm's
default 0.3 pairs Dior with Christian Dior and a great deal else). It finds
{trigram.accepted_found} of the {trigram.accepted_aliases} pairs the policy merged
and misses the acronyms, rebrands and spellings: TXT, Bape, Dior, LOFT, HERMES,
Thierry Mugler, L'Oreal, Thrive, e.l.f. Cosmetics. Its {n(trigram.pairs_proposed_all_brands)}
proposals over the whole catalog are mostly case and punctuation variants,
with pairs that share every trigram such as Boom Boom and Boom. Judging every one of the
{n(estimate.pairs)} pairs instead of the probe's {estimate.probe_decisions} would cost
about ${Math.round(estimate.estimated_cost_usd).toLocaleString('en-US')} at this
run's mean cost per decision; the probe keeps the question affordable.
