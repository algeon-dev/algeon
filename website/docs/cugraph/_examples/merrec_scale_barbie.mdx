A trend analyst agent wants a shortlist of brands whose audience relationship to
Barbie changed between May to June and July to August 2023. A later check of product
names tests whether the change coincides with Barbie-related listings.

Each period has a graph of distinct user-brand likes. The `after_release`
flag is true for July to August likes; the split at 1 July falls 20 days
before the Barbie film opened on 21 July. User vertices are `-(user_id + 1)`
and brand vertices are `brand_id` values; both columns are non-negative and
non-null in this data (users 10 to 58,760,845, brands 0 to 25,814, with
brand 0 excluded). Users therefore occupy negative IDs, and `la.vertex > 0`
keeps only brands.

Personalized PageRank starts from Barbie; ordinary PageRank supplies each
brand's global graph score. Their ratio, `lift`, describes relative graph
affinity: 1 means the two scores are equal. All four calls use the default
options (`alpha` 0.85, `epsilon` 1e-5, `max_iterations` 100), and each
converged before the iteration limit. Lifts from the two periods were
therefore computed to one tolerance. The query ranks the change in that ratio
and requires at least 1,000 fans (distinct users) per brand in both periods.

**Follow-up: change the seed.** A rise in graph affinity could reflect a
broad summer trend rather than Barbie. Running the same statement with
another seed brand tests that. Hot Wheels is a Mattel brand whose collector
audience overlaps Barbie's; Nike is a large brand with no July event. Each
control replaces Barbie's `brand_id` (2595) with the seed's (9877 for Hot
Wheels, 15987 for Nike) in the personalization vertex and the `la.vertex <>`
exclusion; the rest of the statement is unchanged.

With Nike as the seed, none of the four brands appears among the 25 largest
changes. With Hot Wheels, NYX, ALDO and Primark appear with smaller changes
and Amc is absent, consistent with the Mattel collector audience sharing
part of the Barbie movement. The two controls separate the Barbie-specific
movement from a general summer rise; a seasonal baseline over several years
would need data this snapshot lacks. Item attributes are the latest retained
values, and the calendar comparison is not an event-date experiment.
